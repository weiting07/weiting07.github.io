Wei-Ting Wang

Computational Biomedical Informatics · Clinical Phenotyping · Multimodal Data

I am a Research Assistant in the Department of Population Health Sciences, Division of Health Informatics and Artificial Intelligence, at Weill Cornell Medicine, working with Prof. Chang Su. My research focuses on developing computational approaches that integrate longitudinal electronic health records, biomedical knowledge graphs, and single-cell transcriptomic data to investigate disease heterogeneity, patient subtyping, and underlying molecular mechanisms.

Research Interests

My research interests lie in computational phenotyping, patient stratification, and the integration of longitudinal EHR, biomedical knowledge graphs, and single-cell transcriptomic data to investigate disease heterogeneity and mechanisms relevant to precision medicine.

Research perspective

Questions I Work On

How can we define meaningful disease subtypes?

I am interested in whether patterns identified from longitudinal EHR data remain clinically interpretable and reproducible across cohorts—not simply whether an algorithm separates patients into groups.

How do clinical patterns connect to biology?

EHR-derived phenotypes provide a clinical starting point. I use knowledge graph relationships and single-cell evidence to explore which biological mechanisms could explain observed heterogeneity.

What kind of research do I want to pursue in a PhD?

I hope to build rigorous, reusable methods that connect real-world clinical trajectories with molecular evidence, while keeping validation, interpretability, and clinical relevance central to the analysis.
